Say It Out Loud

For birth partners learning the language patterns from His Voice in the Birth Room by Mark Harris, male midwife.

Why this works — briefly and honestly [Read once]

During labour, a woman's analytical thinking quiets and her more instinctive brain takes over. That brain reads the room for signs of safety.

A partner's unmanaged anxiety raises adrenaline and cortisol, which narrows blood vessels and can slow things down. Steady presence and a steady voice are not decoration. They are a biological intervention.

Read this once as context. You do not need to memorise it or overstate it. The work is simply learning to speak steadily when it matters.

The seven language patterns [Reference guide]
1. Say what's true first, then suggest — three, then one

Say three things she can check with her own senses, real and right now, and then one gentle suggestion.

"You can feel the cushion underneath you… hear my voice… notice my hand on your back — and your shoulders can soften."

Never suggest before you have said what is true.

Technical background (collapsed)

Commonly termed "pacing and leading" in communication theory. Grounding sensory perception establishes rapport and lowers cognitive resistance before introducing a suggestion.

2. Permissive, not commanding

Use "you can", "you might notice", "allow".

Never "you must", "relax", "calm down" — an order invites resistance.

Technical background (collapsed)

Often termed gentle or Ericksonian permissive language. An overt command invites the conscious mind to push back; permissive phrases bypass friction.

3. Tone over words

Low, slow, falling at the end. A rising tone asks a question; a falling tone sounds certain, and certainty is what steadies her.

4. The lexical shift — swap the cold word for the truer one

contraction → surge / tightening
pain → power / pressure
failure to progress → taking its time
just relax → let it come

5. Touch before words — a beat ahead

Steady touch starts a beat before you speak, and stays until the sentence ends.

Technical background (collapsed)

Known in neuro-linguistics as anchoring. The physical touch signals safety before the auditory processing begins.

6. Offer a gentle choice — either way, it helps

"Would you rather lean forward on the ball, or lie on your left side?"

She chooses. Either answer moves her the same way.

Technical background (collapsed)

A structured choice where all options are safe and supportive, restoring personal agency without overwhelm.

7. The crisis sequence — when she says "I can't do this any more"

1. Get into her line of sight. Hold her face or hands.
2. Match her — "I can see how powerful this is."
3. Turn it — "and that power is your body opening."
4. Drop your voice and lead — "as it fades, let your hands soften."
5. Hand her back to herself — "You are so powerful. Yield to it."

Tips for Non-Mic Users — Running offline & text practice [Setup & Advice]
1. The app is 100% complete in text-only mode

You do not need a working microphone to master these patterns. The core of this practice is speaking out loud in the physical room with your own voice. The browser recording is merely an optional mirror. Simply speak your line aloud, write what you said into the response field, compare it against the worked example, and complete the self-check. A text session is a complete, uncompromised session.

2. Why microphone access is restricted in local files

Modern web browsers (such as Chrome, Edge, and Safari) enforce strict security boundaries. When an HTML file is opened directly by double-clicking it on your computer (via the file:// protocol), the browser classifies it as an untrusted origin and intentionally blocks microphone capture.

3. Simple step-by-step instructions to enable microphone locally

If you wish to enable audio recording on your computer without an internet connection, you can serve the file using a zero-setup local web server. Browsers treat localhost as a secure context:

  1. Open your terminal: Launch Terminal (macOS / Linux) or Command Prompt / PowerShell (Windows).
  2. Navigate to the folder: Change directory to where say-it-out-loud.html is saved. For example:
    cd ~/Downloads
  3. Start the local server: Run Python's built-in server command:
    python3 -m http.server 8000
    (Or python -m http.server 8000, or npx serve . if Node is installed)
  4. Open the app in your browser: Visit:
    http://localhost:8000/say-it-out-loud.html

Microphone recording and slow playback will now operate with full browser permissions, completely offline.
